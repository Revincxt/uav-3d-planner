# v0.6 model and study-interface plan

## Status and scope

Version 0.6 extends the deterministic predictive study without changing its four planner conditions,
spatial/time lattice, cruise speed, horizon, or work limits. It adds two harder diagnostic cities,
improves D* Lite endpoint anchoring, records dynamic closest approach and discrete waypoint
kinematics, and reorganizes the predictive page around inspectable academic evidence.

The study remains curated, non-preregistered, and non-confirmatory. Scenario acceptance uses only
constructor, geometry, schedule, endpoint, and metadata contracts; planner outcomes are not an
acceptance criterion.

## Frozen study matrix

- 10 scenarios: 1 calibration, 3 demonstration, and 6 diagnostic;
- 4 conditions per scenario: repeated 3D A*, D* Lite reset, D* Lite reuse, and 4D Space-Time A*;
- 40 deterministic mission records;
- the `predictive-space-time-v3` protocol identifier, with the existing schema-v2 serialization and
  continuous space-time collision authority.

The two added diagnostics are:

- `braided-skyway`: 20 staggered towers, one static hospital NFZ, opposing low/high temporary gates,
  and two crossing traffic streams; decision axes are route commitment, altitude, and timing;
- `harbor-switchback`: 20 unequal dock blocks, one static control NFZ, opposing low/high closures,
  and two lateral traffic streams; decision axes are switchback selection, climb timing, and
  opposing traffic.

Both scenes carry four time-dependent hazards and are accepted independently of planner outcomes.

## Measurement contract

Collision certification remains a Boolean continuous space-time audit of the exported dense linear
segments. Version 0.6 adds two descriptive layers:

1. **Dynamic closest approach.** Surface-to-surface separation, witness time and positions,
   obstacle identity/kind, declared safety margin, method, and exactness. Moving-sphere results are
   exact under piecewise-linear relative motion; temporary-cylinder results explicitly report an
   approximate deterministic convex search.
2. **Discrete kinematics.** Segment-average speed, adjacent velocity change, finite-difference
   acceleration proxy, absolute climb rate, and reversal count at a declared 150-degree threshold.
   Waits are reversal boundaries.

Neither layer certifies continuous acceleration, attitude, curvature, jerk, actuator limits,
forecast uncertainty, or operational safety.

## Interface contract

The predictive page should present, in order:

1. study scope and non-confirmatory status;
2. scenario and planner-condition selection;
3. synchronized recorded 3D trajectory replay;
4. raw versus certified path evidence and event state;
5. outcome, closest-approach, smoothing, and discrete-kinematic diagnostics;
6. the four-condition comparison table and artifact provenance.

Visual interpolation must follow the exported certified polyline. UI copy must distinguish collision
certification, exact/approximate separation methods, and diagnostic-only kinematics. Unlike work
units must not be normalized into a planner ranking.

## Release acceptance gates

- the registry and manifest contain exactly 10 accepted scenarios and 40 runs;
- every scene ID, case ID, semantic fingerprint, and run ID is unique and deterministic;
- both extended cities contain 18–22 buildings, at least one static NFZ, and at least three
  time-dependent hazards;
- all exported paths retain exact endpoints, preserve waits, obey the speed ceiling, and pass the
  continuous space-time audit;
- every predictive run carries a closest-approach witness and both raw/output kinematic diagnostics;
- Python reruns reproduce all non-timing fields and JavaScript validates structure, counts, digests,
  diagnostic flags, and display contracts;
- the package, Web package, changelog, methodology, schema documentation, and public provenance
  identify the same v0.6 source revision.

Acceptance depends on these contracts, not on one planner winning. Public-data regeneration and a
release tag occur only after the source revision is frozen.
